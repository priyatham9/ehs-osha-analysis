# Establishment linkage and panel-quality audit

Generated by `python3 -m ehs_osha.panel_audit` from committed tables in `outputs/tables/`; the numbers are in `outputs/tables/panel_linkage_audit.csv`. Do not edit by hand: `tests/test_panel_audit.py` fails if this page differs from the script output.

## Question

The 29.7x screening correction (pooled TRIR 0.134 to 3.983) is computed on filings keyed by `establishment_id`. Before relying on panel results, three things about that key need to be known: how many years each id appears, whether the attributes attached to an id (industry, size band, EIN) are stable, and whether the correction sits in ids that appear only once.

## Data and scope

Only committed outputs are read: `panel_coverage.csv`, `panel_transitions.csv`, `peer_group_persistence.csv`, `quality_by_year.csv`, `quality_overall.csv`, `quality_hours_concentration.csv`, `quality_top50_implausible_hours.csv`. No raw filings are used. Each row of the audit table has a `status`: `answered`, `partial_bound` (the tables give a bound or a proxy, not the quantity), or `not_answerable_from_committed_tables`.

## 2. Do ids change industry or size band between years

**Not answerable from committed tables.** No committed table holds NAICS code or employee size band per id per year (only the top-50 flagged-hours filings carry a NAICS code, one filing each). Counting ids that change industry or size band needs the raw filings. The only committed proxy is group-level: of the NAICS x size-band peer groups present in one year, 95.7% to 98.5% are present in the next (`peer_group_persistence.csv`). That measures whether groups survive, not whether establishments move between them, and a group can survive while its members change.

What the tables do show is drift in other attributes of an id. Among the ids that appear in 2 or more years (the only ones that can change), 51.0% carry more than one EIN, 17.7% more than one company name and 0.7% more than one state. `establishment_id` therefore tracks a site more reliably than a legal filer.

## 3. How much of the 29.7x correction sits in single-appearance establishments

**The share of the correction (hours or cases) from ids that appear once is not answerable from committed tables**, because no table splits hours or cases by years present. What the tables do support:

- By year: 2019 holds 92.6% and 2024 holds 5.4% of pooled flagged hours (`quality_by_year.csv`).
- By filing: the single largest filing holds 88.8% of all reported hours, and the 50 largest flagged filings hold 95.0% (`quality_hours_concentration.csv`, `quality_top50_implausible_hours.csv`). These 50 filings come from 50 distinct ids; whether those ids appear in other years is not in the committed tables.
- By count of flagged filings: from adjacent-year presence alone, at most 32,792 of 57,857 flagged filings (56.7%) belong to ids seen in only one year, and at least 25,065 (43.3%) belong to ids seen in two or more. This is a bound on filing counts, not on the correction: the correction is dominated by a few very large-hours filings, so a count share says little about a hours share.

## Limits

- Presence in adjacent years is the only linkage the transition table records; an id absent in both neighbours may still appear in a non-adjacent year, which is why the single-year figure is an upper bound.
- `establishment_id` stability was not tested against any substitute key.
- Resolving sections 2 and 3 exactly needs the raw filings, for example a per-id table of NAICS, size band and years present.
