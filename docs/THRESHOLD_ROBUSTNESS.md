# Threshold robustness of the implausibility screen

Generated by `python3 -m ehs_osha.threshold_robustness` from committed tables in `outputs/tables/`; the numbers are in `outputs/tables/threshold_robustness_cells.csv` and `outputs/tables/threshold_robustness_summary.csv`. Do not edit by hand: `tests/test_threshold_robustness.py` fails if this page differs from the script output.

## Question

The screen flags a filing when its hours per average employee fall outside a lower and an upper bound. The default (120 and 4,500) is a judgement. Do the two headline conclusions, that screening raises the pooled aggregate TRIR from 0.134 to 3.983 and that about 2.1% of filings are flagged, depend on that judgement?

## Method

This is a specification curve over the one researcher choice the screen has: all 25 pairs of lower bound (100, 120, 200, 250, 400) and upper bound (3,500, 4,000, 4,500, 5,000, 6,000) in `quality_sensitivity_grid.csv`, which the pipeline computed on the real filings. Nothing is re-screened here. Because a filing is flagged by a bound on one variable, the flagged sets are nested along each bound, so subtracting flagged totals between two cells gives exactly the filings that change status between them (the marginal bands below). Flagged hours and cases are recovered as share times the pooled total. The other flags (missing or non-positive hours, missing employees, contradictory counts) do not depend on the bounds and are held fixed.

## Result 1: the aggregate TRIR barely moves

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 3.993 (2.41%) | 3.988 (2.13%) | 3.984 (1.97%) | 3.982 (1.88%) | 3.978 (1.79%) |
| 120 | 3.992 (2.51%) | 3.986 (2.23%) | 3.983 (2.07%) | 3.981 (1.98%) | 3.977 (1.89%) |
| 200 | 3.985 (3.00%) | 3.980 (2.72%) | 3.976 (2.55%) | 3.975 (2.47%) | 3.971 (2.38%) |
| 250 | 3.983 (3.32%) | 3.977 (3.05%) | 3.974 (2.88%) | 3.972 (2.79%) | 3.968 (2.70%) |

- Screened aggregate TRIR runs from 3.963 to 3.993, a range of 0.030. The largest departure from the default (3.983) is 0.50%.
- The unscreened TRIR is 0.134 in every cell, because it does not use the bounds. The screened rate exceeds it in 25 of 25 cells; the ratio runs from 29.56x to 29.79x.
- Averaging out the other bound, the mean screened TRIR changes by 0.015 across the five lower bounds and by 0.015 across the five upper bounds.
- The cell with the lowest screened TRIR is 400-6000; the highest is 100-3500.

## Result 2: the flagged share does move, and that is expected

The flagged share runs from 1.79% (50,180 filings) to 4.49% (125,741 filings), a factor of 2.5 between the extremes. So the statement "about 2% of filings are flagged" is a property of the chosen bounds and should be quoted with the range. What stays put is the weight of what is flagged: flagged filings hold 96.67% to 96.71% of all reported hours and 1.22% to 2.27% of all reported cases in every cell.

## Why: the filings that move carry almost no hours

Moving a bound re-classifies a band of filings. At the default for the other bound:

- Lower bound 120 to 400: 55,448 filings, 1,584,313,187 hours, 76,550 cases, band TRIR 9.663.
- Lower bound 100 to 120: 2,773 filings, 33,306,850 hours, 4,070 cases, band TRIR 24.439.
- Upper bound 4,500 to 3,500 (filings that enter the flagged set): 12,436 filings, 3,362,557,522 hours, 38,909 cases, band TRIR 2.314.
- Upper bound 4,500 to 6,000: 4,904 filings, 1,649,768,341 hours, 14,712 cases, band TRIR 1.784.

The largest band holds 0.018% of the 18,951,698,493,968 pooled hours. The bands' own rates (1.8 to 24.4) differ from the screened aggregate, but they are too small a part of the denominator to move it. The aggregate is set by the few filings with enormous hours, which every cell flags.

## Cross-check with no threshold on hours per employee

Instead of a bound, drop the N filings with the most reported hours (`quality_hours_concentration.csv`) and recompute the aggregate TRIR:

| N dropped | Share of filings | Aggregate TRIR |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.0000% | 1.198 |
| 10 | 0.0004% | 2.456 |
| 100 | 0.0036% | 2.909 |
| 1,000 | 0.0357% | 3.534 |
| 10,000 | 0.3570% | 4.137 |

Dropping the single largest filing lifts the rate from 0.134 to 1.198; dropping the 10,000 largest (0.36% of filings) gives 4.137, the same order of magnitude as the screened 3.983. The trim is cruder than the screen and does not converge to it, but it points the same way: the unscreened rate is low because of a few extreme filings, not because of where a bound was drawn.

## Plain-language interpretation

Whatever reasonable bounds are used in the grid, the screened pooled rate comes out between 3.96 and 3.99 injuries per 100 full-time workers, against 0.13 unscreened. The choice of bounds changes how many filings are called implausible, but not how the aggregate looks, because a handful of filings with impossible hours dominate the denominator and every version of the screen catches them.

## Limits

- **Grid, not universe.** The lower bounds span 100 to 400 and the upper bounds 3,500 to 6,000, as set in `quality.sensitivity_grid`. Bounds outside that region are not tested; the committed tables cannot answer for them.
- **Pooled only.** The grid is committed for the pooled nine years. Per-year behaviour under other bounds is not in the committed tables; only the default is, in `quality_by_year.csv`. The year spread of the unscreened rate is a separate result in the README.
- **One variable.** Only the two hours-per-employee bounds vary. The other flags are fixed and their thresholds (for example employees below 1) are not varied.
- **Robust to thresholds is not the same as correct.** The analysis shows the aggregate is insensitive to where the bounds sit. It does not show that every flagged filing is wrong or that the screened rate equals a true national rate; the data are self-reported and not a sample of the US workforce.
- **Rank-trim check is crude.** Dropping the largest filings by hours also drops legitimately large employers, and it does not target implausible filings specifically. It is a check on mechanism, not a replacement screen.
- **Recovered totals.** Flagged hours and cases are recovered from shares times pooled totals and rounded to integers; differences between cells are exact up to that rounding.
- **No uncertainty interval.** The grid varies a specification, not the sample; no sampling variance is claimed.
